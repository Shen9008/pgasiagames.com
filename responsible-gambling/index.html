What “responsible gambling” means

Responsible gambling means treating betting and gaming as paid entertainment with a fixed time and money budget—like going to a concert or a cinema. You should only wager what you can afford to lose, never borrow to gamble, and never gamble when angry, lonely, or under the influence of alcohol or drugs. If you find yourself hiding your play from family or friends, or feeling anxious about losses, it is time to pause and seek support.

Warning signs that gambling may be a problem

Everyone’s situation is different, but common warning signs include:

  • Spending more money or time than you planned, or chasing losses with bigger bets.
  • Borrowing money, selling possessions, or neglecting bills to fund gambling.
  • Lying to others about how much you gamble or hiding statements and screen time.
  • Feeling irritable or restless when you try to cut down or stop.
  • Gambling to escape stress, sadness, or boredom rather than for enjoyment.
  • Neglecting work, study, relationships, or sleep because of gambling.

If several of these apply to you, consider using our tools immediately and contacting an independent helpline listed below.

Underage gambling

Our services are strictly for adults who meet the legal age in their jurisdiction (often 18 or 21). We use age verification and may request documents before withdrawals. If you are a parent or guardian, use device parental controls, block gambling sites on shared devices, and never save passwords where minors can access them. If you suspect an underage person has used our site, contact us immediately.

Independent support organisations

These organisations offer free, confidential advice—not affiliated with PG Asia Games, but recognised internationally:

  • BeGambleAware — information and support for anyone affected by gambling.
  • GamCare — counselling, forums, and self-help resources.
  • Gambling Therapy — multilingual online support and groups.
  • Gamblers Anonymous — peer support meetings worldwide.

If you are in immediate danger or crisis, contact your local emergency services or a crisis line in your country.

Tips for staying in control

Decide your budget before you deposit. Use deposit limits that match that budget. Take regular breaks; set a timer on your phone if helpful. Never gamble to recover losses. Balance gambling with other hobbies. If you are winning, consider withdrawing part of your balance instead of increasing stakes. Review your play history in your account from time to time.

Parents and shared devices

Keep payment details and account passwords private. Log out after each session on shared computers or tablets. On mobile, use fingerprint or face ID where appropriate and do not allow minors to use your logged-in session. Many operating systems offer screen time or website filters—use them alongside our age checks.

Myths and facts

Myth: “A big win is due because I’ve lost for so long.” Fact: Each spin or hand is independent; past results do not change future odds.

Myth: “I can win back what I lost if I play a bit longer.” Fact: Chasing losses usually increases harm. Set a loss limit and stick to it.

Myth: “Only people with addiction need help.” Fact: Early support—talking to a friend or a helpline—can prevent bigger problems later.
